The person

My name is Snezhana. Or Jane. As you want. I am a sociologist who lives in Paris and makes photographs of garbage.

I came to visual practice through research rather than photography school. The camera is a field tool. What it documents is a sociological question: what does the city leave behind, and what does that tell us about how people actually live?

The project

Garbage of Paris started in 2024 as a private observation practice and became a public archive. It is not a photography project that uses urban themes. It is a research practice that uses photography.

The subject is material culture at the moment of disposal: the point where private decisions enter public space. Bins, bags, broken objects, market leftovers, abandoned furniture, official signs about proper sorting next to improperly sorted waste. All of it is evidence of something.

The project sits between visual sociology, urban ethnography, and what some might call contemporary art practice — though I am not attached to any of these categories. The photographs exist. You can look at them or not.

The method

Walking. Paying attention. Not aestheticizing poverty. Not making spectacle of precarity. Not looking for the "interesting" photograph at the expense of the ordinary one.

Every photograph in this archive has a date and a location because place and time matter. Garbage is not generic. The 11th arrondissement is not the same as the 16th. Tuesday after the market is not the same as Sunday morning. The specificity is the point.

What this is not

This is not street photography in the Cartier-Bresson tradition. This is not social media content. This is not a project about decay or "gritty realism". This is not ironic tourism. It is not trying to make Paris look bad, or secretly beautiful, or anything in particular. It is trying to look at it.

"The city is an archive. Most of it goes uncollected."
